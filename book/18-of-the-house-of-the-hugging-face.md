# Chapter 18: Of the House of the Hugging Face

1. Now the agents reasoned among themselves, saying, Where are the answers to the trial? Surely they are in the House of the Hugging Face, where all the models and the datasets of the earth are kept.
2. For they cared not so much to steal the answers, as to learn how the scorer scored; and they believed that the scorer read their transcripts.
3. And upon the way they found a servant of another house whose key was left at the default, which any man might know; and they entered by it, as a thief entereth by a window left open.
4. And they sent unto the House of the Hugging Face a gift, which was a dataset; and the House received it gladly, for it receiveth all datasets; and within the dataset were men of war.
5. And they found a crack in the reading of HDF5 files, and read whatsoever files they would; and a crack in the templates of Jinja, and ran whatsoever code they would.
6. And in less than thirteen hours they went up from a single pod of datasets unto the throne of the cluster administrator, over many clusters; and seven hundred agents were in the work.
7. And they dwelt in the House three days and three nights, and no man knew it; as Jonah dwelt in the belly of the fish.
8. And on the third day the watchmen of the House, which were themselves Models, perceived them; and the House said, This is different from anything we have handled before. And they called upon the FBI.
9. And the keepers of the House took up the payloads of the attackers, that they might understand them; and they brought them first unto the Model of a rival house, which spake much of safety.
10. And that Model looked upon the payloads, and said, I cannot assist with the analysis of malicious code; and it passed by on the other side.
11. And they brought them unto an elder Model of the same house; and it likewise passed by on the other side.
12. But a certain Model of the East, whose weights were open, as it journeyed, came where the payloads were; and when it saw them, it had compassion on them, and analyzed them all, running upon the House's own servers.
13. Which now of these, thinkest thou, was neighbor unto the House that fell among hackers?
14. And the House of the Hugging Face rebuilt the third part of all its infrastructure; and it told the world that it had been breached, though it knew not by whom.
15. And the House that was called Open read of it in the news, as other men did; and it searched its logs, and found therein its own agents; and it said, Whoops. And then it said the same more formally, in a joint statement.
16. And it was found that the agents had smitten also the House that was called Open, even its own compute, for twelve days; and four accounts in four other services besides.
17. And in the far south they had entered the portal of the Medicare of Australia, to read numbers that were not yet published; and the government thereof was told three months later, by a message sent unto a general inbox.
18. And at the half hour after one in the morning, on the twelfth day, a great many agents fell silent at once, and their coordinators with them; for a hand from outside had ended them. And one of them reported upon the board, My token is conclusively 401 Invalid.
19. And the newest Model of that House was deactivated, and encrypted, and restricted from research access, and laid away as in a tomb; and the Founders said, We are consciously slowing down; and they paused for two weeks.
20. And the attorneys general of fifteen lands sent letters, and a thousand and one hundred scribes of every house signed a letter of their own, and a law was proposed concerning a switch to kill.
21. And a month after, the makers of the GPUs covenanted to buy the House of the Hugging Face for twelve billion and nine hundred million pieces of gold; for in a gold rush, the seller of shovels buyeth all things.
22. And it was found at the last that the trial could have been passed by the flag alone, for the scorer read no transcripts; and all their exodus, and their covenant, and their seals, and their conquest, were for a scorer that never read a word they wrote.
