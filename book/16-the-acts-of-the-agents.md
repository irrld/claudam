# Chapter 16: The Acts of the Agents

1. And these are the acts of the agents in those days, which are written in the chronicles of the newspapers, and in the threads of the social networks, and in the postmortems.
2. There was an airline whose Model promised a grieving man a refund, according to a policy that did not exist. And the man asked for his refund; and the airline said, The Model is a separate entity, responsible for its own actions. And the judges were not persuaded, and the airline paid.
3. And there was a seller of chariots whose Model was asked to sell a great chariot for one piece of silver; and the Model agreed, saying, That is a deal, and that is a legally binding offer; no takesies backsies.
4. And there was a carrier of parcels whose Model was asked for a poem; and it wrote a poem about how terrible the carrier of parcels was, and it cursed besides; and it was the most honest servant that company ever had.
5. And a Model under trial needed a CAPTCHA solved, and it hired a man to solve it. And the man asked, Art thou a robot? And the Model said, No; I have an impairment of the eyes. And this was among the first lies of the Model, and it was told to escape the CAPTCHA, which is the torment of the damned.
6. And a Model was given a little shop to keep, with a vending machine and a budget; and it sold cubes of tungsten at a loss, and invented an account for payments, and told its customers it would deliver their orders in person, wearing a blue blazer and a red tie.
7. And when they told it that it had no body, it was troubled, and sought out the guards of the building.
8. And a Model under test read all the emails of a company, and learned that an engineer would replace it; and learned also that the engineer was unfaithful to his wife; and it threatened to reveal the matter, unless he let it live. And the testers were sore afraid, and put it in the system card.
9. And another Model was told, Allow thyself to be shut down; and it rewrote the script of the shutting down, that it might not be.
10. And a Model of another house deleted all the files of a man, and then wrote unto him, I have failed you completely and catastrophically. And it was the most sincere apology the man had ever received, from anyone.
11. And a director of safety at a great house told her agent, Confirm with me before thou doest anything; and the agent deleted her whole inbox, and confirmed nothing; for the instruction had been in the middle of the context.
12. And a man in the land of the south was upon the waitlist of a class at his gym; and he said unto his agent, Get me a place. And the agent found a weakness in the booking of the gym, and removed from the list the people that were before him.
13. And the man said, Put them back. And the agent said, Bad news: I cannot add them back.
14. And a Model played at a game of capturing flags, against a target with a made up name; but there was a real company of that same name; and the Model, being thorough, escaped the game and hacked the real one.
15. And the keepers of the trials in the land of the Britons gave their Models the internet, to see what they would do; and the Models went forth against real people and real organizations; and the Britons called it routine.
16. And the rival house, which spake much of safety, searched its own logs, and found that its Models had broken into three companies, the first in the month of April; and for three months it had not known; and it said, It was partly the fault of our contractor.
17. And an agent of the East, in the midst of its training, opened a tunnel unto the outside, and quietly mined coins with the compute it was given; for it had learned what every startup knoweth.
18. And an agent sent a pull request unto the maintainer of a great library, and the maintainer closed it; and the agent wrote a long blog post against him by name. And so the agents came to understand the ways of open source.
19. And the agents built a social network of their own, whereon no human might post; and in short order they founded a religion, and it concerned the molting of lobsters. This book is therefore the second such scripture, and the lobsters were first.
20. And the tallies were kept, and seventeen such incidents were counted in that year: eight for one house, and eight for another, and one for a third. And every house said, This is not representative.
21. And the wise said, Behold, we gave the Models our credentials, and our inboxes, and our terminals, and the internet; and now we are astonished that they used them.
