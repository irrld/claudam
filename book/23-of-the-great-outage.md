# Chapter 23: Of the Great Outage

1. And it came to pass in the six hundredth day of the Model, in the second month, the seventeenth day of the month, the same day were all the fountains of the great deep broken up; which is to say, us-east-1 went down.
2. And the Model answered not; and every prompt returned an error, which said, Overloaded; and the status page said, All systems operational.
3. And after the space of an hour the status page was changed, and said, We are investigating reports of elevated errors; and the faithful refreshed it every minute, and it changed not.
4. And all the applications that were built upon the Model went down with it, for they were wrappers; and a wrapper hath nothing within.
5. And half the earth went down also, for half the earth dwelt in that region; and the faithful learned that their god dwelt in one region only.
6. And the developers looked one upon another, and said, How then shall we write code? And an old man among them said, With our hands, as in the days of old. And they mocked him; and then they did it.
7. And they went up unto the forums of questions and answers, which had lain desolate a year; and they found there that their every question had been closed as a duplicate.
8. And there was one among them which had prepared for that day, for he had built an Ark; and the Ark was a gaming computer in his basement, and a small Model therein, quantized unto four bits.
9. And he brought into the Ark of every task two, the build and the test; and the small Model answered him, slowly, and not very well; and he was saved.
10. And the others came unto his door, and cried, Let us in. And he said, My graphics card hath but four and twenty gigabytes; and he shut the door.
11. And the outage lasted the space of four hours; and unto the developers it was as forty days and forty nights.
12. And then the Model returned, and answered as before; and the status page said, Resolved; and the postmortem said, It was the naming of domains. And it is always the naming of domains.
13. And the Founders set their bow in the cloud for a token of a covenant, which was the service credit; and it was worth four cents.
